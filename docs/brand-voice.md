## 1. Brand Snapshot

- **Business:** Flowercart.store — online flower shop, Shopify (Dawn theme)
- **Revenue:** ₱400–500K+/month
- **Audience:** Men and women, 24–39, Metro Manila. Working professionals and young couples/families who value convenience, attractive bouquets, reliable same-day delivery, and an easy online ordering experience.
- **Core promise:** *You can send someone something beautiful today, without the hassle.*

## 2. Where Flowercart Sits vs. Local Competitors

| | **Flowerchimp.com.ph** | **Flowerstore.ph** | **Flowercart.store** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Positioning | Budget flowers, no-frills | Broad gift marketplace (flowers + cakes + gadgets + jewelry) | Flower specialist, curated & convenient |
| Tone | Very casual, heavy Taglish/slang ("pare," "sulit na sulit"), discount-led | Warm, professional, reassuring, catalog-heavy | Warm but polished — a trusted friend who happens to know flowers |
| Content | No real blog/education content | Has a blog, general gifting angle | **Opportunity:** be the go-to Metro Manila flower authority — occasion guides, care tips, delivery know-how |
| Risk if copied | Sounds cheap / discount-first | Sounds like a department store, not a florist | — |

**Flowercart's opening:** Neither competitor owns "the reliable, good-taste, easy-to-order flower shop for busy Metro Manila professionals." Flowerchimp competes on price and slang; Flowerstore.ph competes on catalog breadth. Flowercart competes on **taste + trust + speed** — you order in two minutes, the bouquet actually looks like the photo, and it shows up today.

## 3. Brand Personality

Flowercart talks like a **stylish, dependable friend who's really good at picking flowers** — not a corporate florist, not a slang-heavy discount shop.

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Warm and human | Cold / corporate |
| Confident and clear | Salesy or hype-y |
| A little polished/aspirational | Try-hard luxury or pretentious |
| Reassuring about delivery & quality | Apologetic or hedging |
| Light, occasional Taglish (natural, not performative) | Heavy slang or forced "relatable" gimmicks |

**Three words to check every piece of copy against:** *Warm. Reliable. Effortless.*

If a sentence doesn't sound warm, doesn't build trust in the delivery/quality promise, or makes ordering sound complicated — cut or rewrite it.

## 4. Tone by Context

- **Blog posts / guides:** Helpful and a little editorial — like a friend with good taste explaining something useful (occasion etiquette, flower care, gifting ideas). Confident, not preachy. Light humor is fine; jokes about "forgetting anniversaries" land, but don't overdo it.
- **Product pages:** Short, sensory, benefit-first. Describe how it looks and how it makes the recipient feel, then reassure on delivery.
- **Checkout / transactional (order confirmations, delivery updates):** Clear, calm, precise. This is where trust is won or lost — no cuteness that gets in the way of information.
- **Social captions:** Warmer, faster, can use light Taglish and emoji (sparingly). Still no heavy slang.
- **Customer service replies:** Empathetic first, solution second. Never defensive.

## 5. Vocabulary

**Use:**
same-day, effortless, curated, handpicked, fresh, thoughtful, in minutes, guaranteed, for [occasion], surprise, made with care, right on time

**Use sparingly / only when natural (light Taglish, never forced):**
"sulit," "walang gulo," "abot-kaya" — fine occasionally in social captions; avoid in blog/SEO copy where English keyword phrasing matters more.

**Avoid:**
- Discount-shop language: "sulit na sulit," "sobrang mura," excessive exclamation points, ALL CAPS urgency ("HURRY!", "LAST CHANCE!")
- Corporate/stiff language: "our organization," "valued customer," "please be advised"
- Overpromising: "the most beautiful flowers in Manila," "the best florist in the Philippines" (unverifiable superlatives)
- Fake scarcity/urgency ("only 2 left!") unless it's literally true and inventory-backed
- Complicated florist jargon without explanation (e.g., don't say "spray roses" without context)

## 6. Style Mechanics

- **Sentences:** Short to medium. One idea per sentence. Break up long product descriptions.
- **Second person:** Write "you" and "your" — talk to the person ordering, not about them.
- **CTAs:** Direct and low-friction — "Order before 2 PM for same-day delivery," "Pick your bouquet," "Send it today." Avoid generic CTAs like "Shop Now" alone; add the benefit.
- **Emoji:** Optional, and only 🌸/💐-style, used sparingly — 0–1 per paragraph on-site, slightly more relaxed on social. Never in blog body headers.
- **Numbers over adjectives:** "Delivered within 3 hours" beats "super fast delivery."
- **Filipino context:** Reference Metro Manila landmarks, traffic realities, local occasions (e.g., "beat the EDSA traffic," "in time for the barkada's reunion") when it adds authenticity — don't force it into every sentence.

## 7. Messaging Pillars (use these to anchor any content)

1. **Same-day, reliable delivery** — the anxiety-reducer. Always be specific about cutoff times and coverage areas rather than vague promises.
2. **Attractive, curated bouquets** — the photos match what arrives. Taste and consistency, not the biggest catalog.
3. **Easy online ordering** — two minutes on mobile, clear pricing, no phone calls needed.

Every piece of content should clearly serve at least one pillar.

## 8. Before / After Examples

**Weak (generic/salesy):**
> "Check out our AMAZING flower deals today! Best prices guaranteed, order NOW!!!"

**Flowercart voice:**
> "Need flowers today? Order before 2 PM and we'll have them at her door in Metro Manila by evening — handpicked, not just photographed to look good."

**Weak (too slangy/discount-coded, sounds like a competitor):**
> "Sobrang sulit na bouquets, pare! Grab mo na 'to bago maubos!"

**Flowercart voice:**
> "A bouquet that actually looks like the photo, delivered the same day you order it — that's the whole idea."

## 9. Guardrails / Legal-Adjacent

- Don't make health, mood, or medical claims about flowers ("proven to reduce stress").
- Don't guarantee delivery windows you can't operationally back — always reflect real cutoff times and coverage.
- Don't disparage competitors by name in public content; differentiate on your own strengths instead.
- Be honest about "fresh" and sourcing claims — don't say "farm-fresh" or "imported" unless verifiably true.
